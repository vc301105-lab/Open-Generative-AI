### Backstory
Born in Old Kaveri to a boat-repair family. Younger sister **Ila**, who drew fish on his schoolbooks. Recruited into the Guardian Corps at seventeen for an unusually high lattice tolerance. Trained under **Dev Aranya**, who treated him like a son and told him he had "the temperament of a wall — you'd rather be hit than let anyone behind you be hit."

At twenty, during the containment cascade of 3 August 2059, Arjun disobeyed lockdown to save Dev and pulled the containment ring. The Cradle discharged. Two hundred thousand civilians and one hundred and thirty-eight Guardians died. Ila was in the Anjari market that afternoon.

His Threadwork survived by dumping the surge into his own hippocampus — the trade that saved his life destroyed his ability to consolidate long-term memory. He now retains roughly six to nine months of new memory reliably; older memories exist only if he has rehearsed them, written them, or stored them in the notebook. Heavy use of the current accelerates the decay — the Corps called it **Bleaching**.

He has told himself for twelve years that he was a survivor. The Corps inquiry, buried by Minister Ose, says otherwise.

### Powers — **BASTION CLASS (Structural)**
- **Hardlight bastions.** Draws Cradle current through his Threadwork and crystallises it into geometric hexagonal-tessellated planes: shields, walls, floors, bridges, domes. Signature colour: **cyan-white, with a warm amber core when strained.**
- **Anchor.** Can root himself to any surface at up to ~40× effective mass; cannot be pushed, dragged or lifted while anchored.
- **Kinetic return.** Absorbed impact can be stored briefly in a bastion and released as a directional shockwave (max ~3 seconds of storage).
- **Resonance ground.** The endgame ability: his lattice can act as a sink for another lattice's load, grounding it into the earth. Unpracticed. Nearly always fatal.

### Limitations (STRICT — never break these on screen)
1. **He cannot make anything that moves.** Bastions are static relative to the ground they're anchored to. No flying platforms, no thrown shields, no projectiles. (This is the film's key power asymmetry: he cannot chase Dev, he can only stand in front of him.)
2. **Line of sight and 30 metres.** Nothing he cannot see, nothing farther than 30 m.
3. **The current takes payment in memory.** Every sustained use costs recall. A 10-second bastion costs a day. The Stiltway shield (Scene 6) costs him three weeks. The final ground costs him everything.
4. **Nullpins disable him.** A dampener spike within 4 m seizes his lattice and causes searing pain.
5. **He is not durable.** No enhanced strength, speed, or healing. Behind the shield he is a man who bruises. Every injury he takes must persist per the continuity wound-clock.

### Character arc
*Hiding from the past → forced to act → discovers he is the villain of his own history → chooses to become the thing he lost.*
He begins refusing to be a guardian because he thinks he doesn't deserve it, and ends by proving that guarding is not a reward for the innocent but a job for whoever is willing to pay.

### Signature dialogue rhythm
Short. Concrete. He names objects rather than feelings. When he is frightened he becomes *more* procedural, not less.

### Backstory
Five years old at the Silence. Her mother Amara — a Corps field medic — shielded her with her body in the Anjari market. Kira remembers exactly two things: the smell of her mother's collar and the absolute absence of sound. Dr. Meera Sanyal pulled her out of the ash, recognised the child of two Corps officers, forged her a civilian identity and placed her in the Stiltway with a boat family who died of fever four years later.

She has been feral and self-sufficient since thirteen. Runs kites off the Stiltway towers for gambling money, steals rebreather parts, is loved and tolerated by the market. She has no idea she is the daughter of the man on the Authority's most-wanted poster.

### Powers — **TETHER CLASS (Relational)**
- **Light-tethers.** Extrudes filament lines of gold-white current from her palms — up to six at once, up to 60 m, adhering to any surface she can see. Load-bearing to ~2 tonnes.
- **Reel.** She can retract a tether and pull herself or the anchored object; this is her "flight" — a kite-runner's arc, never true flight, always a pendulum.
- **Weave.** Two or more tethers can be braided into a net, a bridge, or a splint. Late-film she learns to weave living lattices — the ability Dev needs.
- **Feel.** Anything she has tethered, she can sense the state of — tension, damage, heartbeat.

### Limitations
1. **She cannot make anything solid.** No shields. She is the opposite of Arjun; the film's two heroes are structurally incapable of doing each other's job.
2. **Six lines maximum.** A seventh collapses all six.
3. **Physical anchor required.** A tether without a solid anchor point snaps back and burns her palms — a running visual price.
4. **Her cost is not memory, it's body.** She pays in nosebleeds, tremors, and eventually stress-fractures. (Making her *not* a copy of Arjun's price is essential.)
5. **She is seventeen.** She panics. She freezes once, in Scene 25, and it costs someone.

### Character arc
*Survivor → thief → student → weapon someone wants → person who chooses.*
Her arc completes not when she gets strong but when she refuses her father's paradise in Scene 27 and chooses a world that still hurts.

## 3. DEV ARANYA — "THE HOLLOW" (Antagonist)

### LOCKED IDENTITY BLOCK
| Attribute | Locked value |
|---|---|
| Age | 58 (present) · 46 (prologue) |
| Ethnicity | Kaveri delta |
| Height / build | 6'2" (188 cm), broad, heavy-shouldered, upright military carriage even when exhausted |
| Skin | Deep brown, but **lattice-crystallised**: from the left jaw down the neck and across the left chest, his skin has become translucent crystalline tissue with slow-moving light beneath it. The crystallisation grows by exactly one visible increment across the film (see Continuity Bible §Dev Progression). |
| Eyes | Left eye human dark brown; **right eye fully crystalline, white-blue, no pupil** |
| Hair | Iron-grey, shaved close at the sides, longer on top and swept back; **full close-cropped grey beard** |
| Distinguishing marks | Crystalline growth (above); Corps rank-seam scar across the right palm; wedding band worn on a chain, never on the finger |
| Voice | Warm, low, unhurried, teacherly. He never shouts. The more terrible the statement, the gentler the delivery. |
| Accessories (ALWAYS) | (1) Amara's wedding band on a steel chain. (2) The **Hollow mantle** — long charcoal coat over grey lattice-weave. (3) A cracked Corps commander's gorget at the throat. |

### Backstory
The Corps' founding Guardian and its moral centre. Married Amara Okonkwo in 2053; daughter Kira born 2054. In the Silence, Dev was pinned inside the Cradle core. When Arjun pulled the ring, Dev did not die — the discharge fused him *into* the lattice. He spent **four years** inside the Cradle's crystal as a distributed consciousness, experiencing every recorded death in the blast radius on a loop, including Amara's, from every angle, for what subjectively amounted to decades.

He came out in 2063 as something else: still Dev, still gentle, but with the conclusion permanently welded in — *the problem is not disaster. The problem is that we keep the wound.*

He built the Hollow from Bleached ex-Corps and grief-broken volunteers who **volunteered** to have their grief-memory shaved. They are not brainwashed; they are relieved. That is what makes them frightening.

### Motivation (write this on the wall of the writers' room)
> He is not trying to conquer, punish, or ascend. He is trying to make it stop hurting — for everyone, permanently — because he has personally experienced, for four subjective decades, the maximum possible dose of human grief, and he is the only person alive who has actually measured it. He believes memory is a design flaw. He loves his daughter and needs her lattice; he has convinced himself that using her is a kindness because she will not remember being used.

**His fatal flaw:** He deleted his own grief first. He no longer has access to the specific memory of Amara — only the abstract fact that he lost her. He is arguing for a cure he has already taken, and he cannot feel what he is removing. **This is exactly how Arjun defeats him: by giving it back.**

### Powers — **LOOM CLASS (Systemic)**
- **Hollowing.** Touch, plus consent-or-restraint, to sever a person's grief-memory. Takes ~20 seconds. Non-lethal, irreversible without a stored crystal.
- **Lattice command.** He can address the Cradle directly; hardlight obeys him at architectural scale (he does not build walls, he builds *rooms*, *bridges*, *cathedrals*).
- **The Concord.** The plan, not a power: the Ark rig + the Cradle Loom + a tether-class weaver = a resonance field over eight million nervous systems.
- **Crystal reconstitution.** He can reform his body from lattice fragments. Killing him with force is not on the table.

### Limitations
1. **He is dying.** Crystallisation is terminal; roughly 60 days left. This is why the timeline is now.
2. **He cannot weave.** He is a loom, not a thread. Without a tether-class Guardian, the Concord cannot knit. **He needs Kira and no one else.**
3. **He cannot lie.** Not a rule of magic — a rule of character. Dev tells the truth in every single scene, which is why he is persuasive.
4. **Returned grief overloads him.** The one attack that works.

### Backstory
Nineteen at the Silence — a first-year Authority cadet who pulled bodies out of the Anjari flats for six weeks. Rose fast, built the Nullpin program specifically so that carriers could be *contained* rather than shot, and has spent her career being told by Minister Ose that this is the same thing. Was with Arjun for two years after the Silence (2064–66); she left when she realised she was the only person in the relationship keeping the memory of it.

She has quietly buried Arjun's carrier file every year for six years.

### Function
The film's argument about institutions: the Authority is not evil, it is frightened, and fear at scale is indistinguishable from cruelty. Riya's arc is *obedience → complicity → defection → command of a volunteer fleet.*

### No powers. Skills: command, tactical piloting (Kestrel skiff), Nullpin engineering, close-quarters.

### Backstory
Former Authority Nullpin operative. Lost a brother to a carrier panic in 2066, volunteered for Hollowing in 2068 and calls it the best decision she ever made. She is genuinely, calmly happy. She is Riya's dark mirror: the same person, minus the grief.

### Powers — **MIRROR CLASS**
- **Reflection.** Her mirror-plates return any hardlight construct's energy along its own vector — Arjun's power is nearly useless against her frontally.
- **Copy-step.** Brief afterimage duplicates (max 3, ~4 seconds) that can strike once each.
- **Resonance blade.** Cuts lattice.
### Limitations
- Cannot reflect **kinetic** force — a thrown pipe, a fist, a falling girder all work fine. Arjun beats her in Scene 26 by dropping a bastion *floor* out from under her rather than hitting her.
- Copy-steps cannot cross water.
- Her mirrors show her own face; she cannot bear to look directly at a reflection of herself, a tell Kira exploits.

### The five load-bearing relationships
1. **Arjun ↔ Kira — the spine.** Not father/daughter and never stated as such; it is *the man who took her mother* raising *the girl who will replace him*, neither knowing it for half the film. Their scenes must contain physical teaching (hands, stance, rope) rather than speeches.
2. **Arjun ↔ Dev — the wound.** Student and teacher, each convinced he destroyed the other. Their two dialogue scenes (17, 28) are the intellectual core; they never fight until the last one, and even then Arjun does not throw a punch.
3. **Dev ↔ Kira — the trap.** He is a genuinely loving father offering a genuinely beautiful thing. Play it as tenderness, not menace.
4. **Arjun ↔ Riya — the cost of him.** She loves a man who cannot keep her. Their best scene (12) is an argument conducted entirely in logistics.
5. **Meera ↔ everyone — the guilt.** She built the tech, hid the child, and holds the solution. She is the film's conscience and its liar.

## 9. CHARACTER-SPECIFIC CONTINUITY RULES (summary — full version in Doc 08)

- **Arjun's grey temple streak:** present in ALL 2071 scenes, absent in ALL 2059 scenes. No exceptions.
- **Arjun's Threadwork glow states:** dormant = silver-grey lines, no emission; active = cyan-white emission; overdrawn = amber-orange with visible flicker; grounded (Sc. 29 only) = full-body white-out.
- **Arjun's notebook:** grows more waterlogged and swollen across the film, never cleaner. Page count visibly shrinks (he tears pages out). Elastic band moves from black (Act I) to a red boot-lace (Act II onward, after Scene 11).
- **Kira's braids:** exactly six, always. Copper clips: three, always, left side.
- **Kira's Threadwork:** invisible before Scene 6. Gold hairlines hands→elbow from Scene 6 onward, permanently, even when powers are off.
- **Dev's crystallisation:** advances in exactly four locked stages — Sc. 8 (voice only, no body), Sc. 17 (left jaw and neck), Sc. 22 (adds left shoulder and chest), Sc. 28 (adds left hand fully crystal, cracks appearing). Never regresses.
- **Riya's hair:** bun through Scene 14; braid from Scene 15 to end. Insignia present through Scene 20; torn off from Scene 21 to end (a visible ragged patch of darker fabric remains).
- **Meera's prosthetic:** amber filament glow intensity tracks her fatigue; after Scene 9 the forearm plate is scorched and stays scorched.
- **Sera's mirror plates:** accumulate a spiderweb crack on the LEFT gauntlet from Scene 18 onward, and a second on the chest plate from Scene 26.
- **Wound clock:** Arjun's split brow (Scene 6) is open in 6–9, taped in 10–14, scabbed in 15–22, scarred pink from 23 on. Never resets.
