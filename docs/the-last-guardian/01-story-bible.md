## 1. LOGLINE

**Primary logline**
> Twelve years after a catastrophe erased a coastline and every super-powered Guardian but one, a memory-losing salvage diver must train the last child to inherit his power — and stop the mentor he thought he killed from erasing the grief of all humanity, one mind at a time.

**Alternate (marketing, 25 words)**
> The last Guardian on Earth is forgetting who he is. To save eight million minds, he must spend the only thing he has left: his memory.

**Theme statement**
> *Grief is the price of love, and memory is the price of guarding anything at all.* The villain offers a world without pain by deleting the past; the hero proves that a person willing to remember for others is what a guardian actually is.

**Central dramatic question**
Will Arjun Vedh recover the memory of what he did — and then willingly burn every memory he has — to give a seventeen-year-old girl a future he will never be able to remember?

## 2. COMPLETE STORY (Full narrative synopsis)

### The world before
In 2059, the world's deepest tidal-energy excavation beneath the Kaveri Delta struck something that was not geology: **the Cradle**, a kilometre-wide lattice of self-organising crystal older than the coastline, humming at a frequency that human nervous systems could carry. The Coastal Authority built the **Guardian Corps** — one hundred and forty volunteers surgically threaded with lattice filament (**Threadwork**) so they could draw the Cradle's current and shape it: hardlight bastions, gravity anchors, storm-scale force. For six years the Corps held back sea surges, quenched reactor fires and became the most beloved institution on Earth.

### The Silence
On 3 August 2059, during a containment cascade at the Cradle, twenty-year-old cadet **Arjun Vedh** watched his mentor **Dev Aranya** — the Corps' greatest Guardian — get pinned inside the collapsing core. Against every protocol, Arjun pulled the containment ring to reach him. The Cradle discharged. A soundless white bloom rolled ninety kilometres up the coast. Two hundred thousand civilians died standing up. One hundred and thirty-eight Guardians burned out in one breath. The coast where it landed — the **Anjari Salt Flats** — has been bleached, windless and silent ever since.

Two people walked out. Arjun, whose Threadwork survived by dumping the surge into his own hippocampus — permanently damaging his ability to store memory. And Dev, who was never found.

The official record blamed a structural fault. Arjun, who remembers nothing of the final ninety seconds, has spent twelve years believing he is a survivor. He is not. He is the cause.

### Present day — 2071, New Kaveri
The delta megacity is a vertical slum-forest of stilt towers, sea-walls and drowned streets under a monsoon that never fully stops. Guardians are illegal. The Authority hunts residual Threadwork carriers with **Nullpins** — dampener spikes that seize a lattice and cook it.

Arjun, 32, works salvage in the flooded ruins of Old Kaveri, pulling copper out of the **Drowned Cathedral** — the sunken transit hall where the Corps once mustered. He keeps his power dead. He keeps a leather notebook of everything he must not forget: *your sister's name was Ila. You take chai with too much sugar. You are not allowed to use the current.* He carries a brass **anchor-stone** on his dive harness — a Corps memory-crystal that has never opened.

Two things happen in one week.

**One:** on a deep dive, Arjun's anchor-stone wakes. It has been *pinged* — something in the Cradle is calling Corps hardware home.

**Two:** a girl steals his rebreather regulator in the Stiltway market. **Kira Okonkwo**, 17, kite-runner, orphan, mouth like a blade. When white-armoured harvesters — the **Hollow** — sweep the Stiltway hunting "carriers", they come for *her*. Kira's hands ignite cyan-white in front of five hundred people. Arjun breaks twelve years of discipline, raises a bastion over a collapsing walkway, and saves four hundred lives on camera.

By morning, the last Guardian is the most-wanted man in the delta, and **Commander Riya Sen** of the Coastal Authority — who used to love him, who buried his file to protect him — recognises the shape of his shield on the news.

### The call
At **Ward 12**, the clinic of **Dr. Meera Sanyal** — the neuro-engineer who built Arjun's stabiliser and lost her left arm in the Silence — the anchor-stone finally opens. Inside is a Corps recording twelve years old, and a voice that should be dead: *"Arjun. When you can hear this, it means I've finished. Come home. I've found the way to make it stop hurting."*

Dev Aranya is alive.

Arjun refuses. He is not a Guardian, he is a man with a notebook. Then the Hollow burn Ward 12 to get at Kira, Meera takes a resonance blade through the shoulder, and refusal stops being available. Arjun takes the girl and runs.

### The bleached coast
They cross the Tidewall and hide at the Anjari Salt Flats — the dead white silence Arjun caused, though he still doesn't know it. There, in the only place on Earth where the Cradle's current runs clean, he teaches Kira the three laws he was taught: *anchor before you build; never shield what you can move; the current takes payment in you.* Her power is not his. Where Arjun makes walls, Kira makes **tethers** — lines of light that bind, pull, connect. She flies them like kite-lines.

Riya finds them. She has orders to bring in both. Instead she trades: her silence for the truth about what Dev is building. The three of them break into the **Bastion Spire** archive and find the Corps' sealed inquiry — and Arjun watches ninety seconds of helmet footage he has never seen.

He sees himself pull the ring.

**Midpoint.** Arjun's memory returns like a wound reopening. He is not the survivor of the Silence. He is its author. Two hundred thousand people, one hundred and thirty-eight Guardians, and the man now calling himself the Hollow — all of it is his.

### The Concord
Dev makes contact through the Cradle: a mind-space parley in a sunlit version of the coast as it was, where nothing is drowned and nobody is dead. His plan is called **the Concord**. Using the Cradle and a converted sea-rig — **the Ark** — he will broadcast a resonance that laces every human nervous system into one field and severs the individual capacity for grief-memory. No war, no vengeance, no widows. One mind, no scars. He is not a sadist; he is the most tired man alive. He watched his wife **Amara Okonkwo** die in the Silence while holding their five-year-old daughter, and he has spent twelve crystalline years deciding that the mistake was not the accident — it was memory itself.

The daughter is Kira. Meera hid her. Meera never told either of them.

**Dev needs Kira** — a *tether*-class Guardian is the only lattice that can knit eight million nervous systems into one weave. **Sera Vance / Echo-One**, Dev's mirror-powered enforcer, takes the girl off a rooftop kite-field while Arjun is on his knees inside his own returning memory.

### Low point
Arjun burns too much current chasing them and Bleaches badly: he loses Riya's name, the clinic, his sister's face. He wakes with a notebook he can't parse and a stranger crying in front of him. Meera, one-armed and stitched, tells him the truth she has been carrying: the stabiliser can be reversed into an **anchor** — Arjun can dump the Concord's entire resonance load through his own lattice and ground it. It will work. It will also strip every memory he has ever stored, permanently. He will survive as a man with no past at all.

He writes one last page in the notebook — instructions to a stranger who will be himself — and closes it.

### Third act
Riya breaks with the Authority, brings six Kestrel skiffs and the Stiltway's boat people through a category-five storm. Aboard the Ark, Dev shows Kira the Concord: her mother, alive, in a world with no grief. She almost takes it. What stops her is small — Arjun's voice on a memory-crystal, reading out loud the things he is afraid to forget, including her name, listed third.

Kira refuses her father. Dev, weeping, initiates the Concord anyway; the resonance climbs the coast and eight million people begin to go quiet in the same second.

Arjun reaches the Cradle Loom. He does not fight Dev with force — Dev is stronger, and always was. He fights him with the one thing the Hollow deleted: he gives Dev back his memory of Amara, uncut, the whole unbearable thing, using the anchor-stone as a conduit. Dev breaks. In the last seconds he turns the Loom's polarity himself so that his daughter will not have to.

But the load still has to go somewhere. Arjun grounds it. Every filament in his body lights, the resonance pours through him into the drowned city, and the Concord dies. Dev, hollow lattice collapsing, sees Kira's face clearly for the first time in twelve years and says her name correctly. Then he is ash and white light. The storm breaks. For the first time in the film, there is sky.

### Ending
Four months later. The Stiltway is rebuilding. Arjun is alive, sitting on a jetty, calm and unhurried and completely empty — he does not know Riya, or Meera, or his own name until he checks. Kira, in a repaired Corps mantle, is the delta's Guardian now, hauling girders with tethers of light while children run under her.

Riya sits down and hands him the notebook. He reads the last page: *"You did a good thing. You won't remember it. That's allowed."*

Kira puts a kite line in his hand. He looks up, and the wind takes it, and something in his face moves — not memory, but the shape of it.

**TITLE CARD: THE LAST GUARDIAN.**
Under it, smaller: *the first of the next.*

**Structural notes for the director**
- Act I is shot handheld and tight (the film is hiding, like Arjun). Act II opens up to locked-off wides (the truth is exposed). Act III returns to handheld but at wide focal lengths — chaos with scale.
- The Silence prologue is *withheld* in full: Scene 1 cuts away 4 seconds before Arjun's hand reaches the ring. Scene 15 replays the identical shot list and continues past the cut. The two sequences must match frame-for-frame in blocking, wardrobe and lighting — this is the single most important continuity obligation in the film.
- Kira's kite is planted in Scene 3, paid off in Scene 18 (taken), Scene 27 (line as weapon), Scene 30 (final image).
- The notebook is planted Scene 5, used Scene 19, written Scene 23, read aloud Scene 27, delivered Scene 30.

## 4. THEMATIC & TONAL RULES

1. **No quips during death.** The film may be warm, never flippant when someone is dying.
2. **Powers always cost.** Every use of the current is followed within two scenes by a visible price (nosebleed, tremor, a forgotten name, a wrong name).
3. **The villain is never wrong about the problem, only about the solution.** Dev's diagnosis of human grief must be allowed to land.
4. **The city is a character.** Every action beat must endanger and be answered by ordinary residents — boat people, kite runners, market vendors.
5. **One clear sky.** Rain, cloud or ash in every scene except the final 90 seconds.
